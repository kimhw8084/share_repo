# Gold Standard Output Patterns

Version: 1.0  
Use this file as compact guidance for what excellent output should look like. These are patterns, not full reports.

## Objective

A gold standard answer should feel like it already passed multiple expert review rounds. It should connect evidence, graph structure, money flow, bottlenecks, simulation, and investment themes.

## Universal gold-standard trace

For top investment themes, use this chain:

`Evidence → Signal → Node/Edge → Money-flow/Bottleneck → Investment Theme → Catalyst → Falsifier`

Example format:

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| Evidence | Recent source or clearly labeled inference |
| Signal | Demand, supply, capex, margin, regulation, or technology change |
| Node/Edge | Which graph node or edge changes |
| Money-flow/Bottleneck | Who pays, who captures value, or what constraint tightens |
| Investment Theme | Specific investable thesis |
| Catalyst | What can reprice it |
| Falsifier | What would prove it wrong |

## Gold pattern: AI infrastructure

Weak answer:

`AI is growing, so GPUs and data centers are attractive.`

Gold-standard pattern:

`Enterprise and cloud AI demand increases sustained compute usage. That strengthens the edge from AI workloads to data center capacity and power. Money flows from enterprise AI budgets to cloud/API providers, then into hyperscaler capex for GPUs, HBM, networking, cooling, and power infrastructure. The hidden bottleneck may shift from chips alone to power-ready sites, interconnection queues, transformers, switchgear, liquid cooling, and optical networking.`

Theme shape:

- Theme: power-ready AI infrastructure and grid equipment
- Money logic: AI demand converts into data center capex and grid/power bottlenecks
- Catalyst: rising capex guidance, power constraints, lead-time expansion
- Falsifier: AI workload growth slows or power capacity expands faster than expected

## Gold pattern: GLP-1 drugs

Weak answer:

`GLP-1 drugs are popular, so pharma companies benefit.`

Gold-standard pattern:

`Patient demand is only one part of the graph. Real adoption depends on clinical outcomes, side effects, prescriber behavior, payer reimbursement, employer/government coverage, pharmacy distribution, API manufacturing, fill-finish capacity, and injector supply. Money flows from payers/employers/governments/patients through pharmacy and provider channels to drug manufacturers, while bottlenecks may appear in manufacturing capacity, reimbursement limits, adherence, and delivery devices.`

Theme shape:

- Theme: capacity and delivery-device leverage around GLP-1 scaling
- Money logic: covered demand converts into recurring drug revenue only if manufacturing and reimbursement scale
- Catalyst: coverage expansion, capacity guidance, trial results
- Falsifier: payer restrictions, adherence problems, safety issues, or competing therapies reduce growth

## Gold pattern: copper mining

Weak answer:

`Copper benefits from electrification.`

Gold-standard pattern:

`Copper demand from grid buildout, EVs, construction, and industrial activity must be compared against mine supply, ore grade decline, permitting delays, concentrate availability, smelting/refining capacity, treatment charges, inventories, and China demand. Money flow depends on whether price rises accrue to miners, smelters, traders, recyclers, or downstream users.`

Theme shape:

- Theme: scarce long-cycle copper supply
- Money logic: demand growth meets slow mine permitting and ore-grade pressure
- Catalyst: inventory drawdown, treatment-charge movement, grid capex, mine disruption
- Falsifier: China demand weakens, substitution accelerates, or new supply arrives faster than expected

## Gold pattern: nuclear power

Weak answer:

`Nuclear is clean energy, so it should grow.`

Gold-standard pattern:

`Nuclear demand is tied to grid reliability, decarbonization, industrial power, and data center demand, but value depends on reactor construction risk, regulatory approval, safety, uranium, conversion, enrichment, fuel fabrication, waste handling, utility rate-base rules, power purchase agreements, and government support.`

Theme shape:

- Theme: regulated nuclear fuel-cycle and reliability infrastructure
- Money logic: reliability demand and policy support may move money to utilities, fuel-cycle suppliers, and approved reactor projects
- Catalyst: PPA announcements, licensing progress, subsidy/loan support, uranium/fuel contracting
- Falsifier: cost overruns, delays, public opposition, or cheaper alternatives dominate

## Gold pattern: private credit

Weak answer:

`Private credit is growing because banks lend less.`

Gold-standard pattern:

`Private credit depends on borrower demand, bank regulation, investor fundraising, direct lending spreads, base interest rates, default rates, covenants, liquidity, valuation marks, and refinancing conditions. Money flows from pensions/insurers/wealth/endowments into private credit funds, then into middle-market or sponsor-backed borrowers. The key investment question is whether spread income compensates for default, liquidity, and valuation risk.`

Theme shape:

- Theme: disciplined direct lending managers with pricing power and strong underwriting
- Money logic: bank retrenchment and investor demand can expand spreads/originations, but defaults and liquidity risk determine realized returns
- Catalyst: bank regulation, fundraising flows, default data, non-accrual trends
- Falsifier: defaults rise faster than spreads compensate or fundraising/liquidity dries up

## Final-answer gold standard

The final answer should:

- Start with a decisive executive conclusion.
- Teach the industry from zero.
- Show the graph and final customers.
- Explain who pays and who captures value.
- Distinguish evidence from inference.
- Identify obvious and hidden bottlenecks.
- Simulate second-order effects.
- Rank investment themes with catalysts and falsifiers.
- End with a reusable snapshot.
