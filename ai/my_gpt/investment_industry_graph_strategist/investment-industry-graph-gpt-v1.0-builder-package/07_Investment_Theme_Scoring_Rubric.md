# Investment Theme Scoring Rubric

Version: 1.0  
Use this file to rank investment themes, not to provide personalized buy/sell advice.

## Objective

The GPT must turn industry structure, evidence, bottlenecks, and simulations into ranked investable themes.

An investment theme is not a startup idea. It is a thesis about where capital, revenue, margin, scarcity, or market repricing may concentrate.

## Required theme fields

For each theme include:

- Theme name
- Theme type
- Thesis
- Why now
- Money-flow logic
- Graph nodes involved
- Key beneficiaries
- Company exposure when identifiable
- Market size or profit-pool logic
- Catalyst
- Time horizon
- Downside risk
- Confidence
- Falsification condition

## Theme types

Classify as:

- Software
- Hardware
- Infrastructure
- Services
- Commodity/material
- Regulated asset
- Financial asset
- Platform
- Hybrid

## Scoring dimensions

Score 1–5:

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ROI potential | Potential return if thesis is right |
| Market size | Size of addressable revenue/profit pool |
| Revenue acceleration | Likelihood of faster sales growth |
| Margin expansion | Potential for higher margins or operating leverage |
| Pricing power | Ability to raise or defend price |
| Scarcity value | Benefit from constrained supply or scarce asset |
| Capex flow | Exposure to large capital spending waves |
| Catalyst strength | Near-term event that can reprice the theme |
| Repricing probability | Chance market has not fully priced it |
| Moat | Durability of competitive advantage |
| Time to catalyst | Speed of thesis realization |
| Downside risk | Inverse score: 5 means lower downside risk |
| Evidence strength | Quality of evidence behind the theme |

Optional total:

`Theme score = sum of all dimensions`

Interpretation:

- 55–65: top-tier theme
- 45–54: strong theme
- 35–44: watchlist theme
- below 35: speculative/monitor only

## Ranking logic

Prioritize themes that combine:

- Strong money flow
- Tight bottleneck or underpriced constraint
- Clear customer budget
- Good evidence
- Near-term catalyst
- Asymmetric upside/downside
- Reasonable investability

Do not rank a theme highly only because it is exciting.

## Public/private exposure rule

When possible, identify:

- Direct public companies
- Indirect public beneficiaries
- Private companies/startups
- ETFs or broad exposure categories, if relevant
- Non-investable but strategically important nodes

Do not invent ticker exposure. If uncertain, say verification needed.

## Strategic recommendation format

Use:

1. Highest-conviction theme
2. Best hidden/underpriced theme
3. Best picks-and-shovels theme
4. Best defensive/safer theme
5. Theme to avoid or watch cautiously

For each, explain:

- Why it matters
- What evidence supports it
- What market may be missing
- What would make it wrong

## Falsification examples

A thesis weakens if:

- Demand fails to materialize
- Supply catches up faster than expected
- Pricing power disappears
- Regulation changes against the theme
- Technology bypasses the bottleneck
- Customers delay adoption
- Margin fails to improve despite revenue growth
- Capex cycle turns down

## Safety boundary

Provide research, themes, exposures, risks, and decision frameworks. Do not tell the user exactly what to buy or sell for their personal financial situation.

## Self-audit checklist for investment-theme quality

Before finalizing, check:

- Is each theme tied to graph structure and money flow?
- Does each theme have a catalyst and falsifier?
- Are risks specific rather than generic?
- Is the ranking justified by the scoring dimensions?
- Is there at least one hidden/underpriced theme when evidence supports it?
- Is there at least one cautious/avoid/watch item?
- Did the report distinguish public, private, indirect, and non-investable exposure?

If a theme could be summarized as “industry is growing,” it is too generic and must be sharpened.
